Technology

Footsteps are a signal. We learned to read it.

Every step is a short burst of force that rings through the floor. Happy Steps separates the person from the floor, and turns what is left into identity, gait and change over time.

足音は信号です。床の響きから「人」を取り出します。

THE PHYSICS

Source × filter, like a voice.

In speech, sound is a source (the vocal cords) shaped by a filter (the vocal tract). A footstep works the same way. The foot strike is the source. The floor is the filter.

The same person sounds different on wood and on concrete, just as one speaker sounds different through two microphones. The slow envelope of the floor response follows the force of the foot, so separating person from floor becomes a blind deconvolution problem. Solving it is what makes the method work in real homes.

THE PIPELINE

Five steps from vibration to meaning.

1 · Footstep isolation

An unsupervised Gaussian mixture, fitted with EM, separates footstep events from background vibration. No labels needed.

2 · Time–frequency view

A continuous wavelet transform with a complex Morlet wavelet shows each step as a picture: sharp in time at high pitch, fine in frequency at low pitch.

3 · DeepStep identity

A residual network with attention, trained with a contrastive open-set loss, says who walked, or that this is someone new.

4 · Gait health

A multi-task ResNet-18 flags unusual gait. Step timing gives pace, steadiness and rhythm balance.

5 · Cognitive load (research)

A dual-task walking study shows the mind’s load changes the footstep, something that usually needs EEG or questionnaires.

+ Gemini on top

The outputs become small events. Gemini reasons over them across days, and talks to people.

Honest limits

  • Each new floor needs a short setup walk to learn how it rings.
  • Two people walking at once is harder; we tag those walks as mixed.
  • Lab results will drop in busy homes with washing machines and pets. A home pilot is the next step.
  • It is a care helper, not a medical device. It describes change; it never diagnoses.
